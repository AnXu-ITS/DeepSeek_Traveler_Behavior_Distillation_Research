# Singapore Urban Mobility and Travel Choice Study — English instrument

English presentation of the retained bilingual questionnaire. Question order, options, numerical scenario values and pair identifiers are preserved. Duplicate bilingual lines are consolidated. This document is not a revised questionnaire and does not alter original responses.

## Introduction

Thank you for participating in this academic study of everyday travel choices and transport preferences among people who live, work or study in Singapore. The study examines decisions under changes in weather, transit access, delays, fares and roadworks, to inform research on sustainable and traveler-oriented transport systems.

Estimated completion time: **4–6 minutes**. Participation is voluntary and you may withdraw while completing the questionnaire. The retained instrument states that it is anonymous, does not collect names, NRIC/FIN numbers, telephone numbers, precise addresses or IP-based personal identifiers, and uses aggregate statistics for academic research. This reproduces the questionnaire's privacy statement; it is not an independent audit of platform telemetry. The researcher contact is provided in the manuscript.

## Section 1: informed consent and eligibility

**1. Informed consent confirmation (required).** Please confirm that you are at least 18 years old, understand the study information and voluntarily agree to participate.

- Yes, I understand and agree to participate → continue.
- No, I do not wish to participate → end the survey.

**2. Singapore residence status (required).** This study concerns daily travel in Singapore. Please indicate your current residency status.

- Singapore Citizen / Permanent Resident (PR)

- Student Pass holder studying in Singapore
- Long-Term Visit Pass / other long-term resident (including LTVP / Dependant's Pass)
- Tourist / short-term visitor → end the survey

**3. Age group (required).** 18–24; 25–34; 35–44; 45–64; 65 years and above.

## Section 3: ten stated-choice travel scenarios

Imagine a regular weekday trip in Singapore. Each task specifies distance, weather and four alternatives with total travel time, cost and PT transfer/access conditions. Read the conditions and choose the mode you would most likely use.

Every task has the same single-choice options: **Car (driving / taxi); Public transport (MRT / bus); Bicycle; Walk.** Driving and taxi costs are displayed separately within the car option. All bicycle and walking costs are SGD 0.00. “Time” below is door-to-door minutes and “access” is the first walk to the PT stop.

## Design appendix: paired contrasts

This appendix explains the retained designer's intended comparisons; these hypotheses are not measured findings. Baseline and intervention tasks are nonadjacent in the displayed order.

| Pair | Baseline → intervention | Task positions | Intended question |
|---|---|---|---|
| W: weather | W0 → W1 | 3 → 7 | Does heavy rain shift exposed walking/cycling toward sheltered modes? |
| A: accessibility | A0 → A1 | 1 → 6 | How do longer access walking and more transfers change PT choice? |
| F: fare | F0 → F1 | 8 → 2 | How sensitive are stated choices to a 50% PT fare increase? |
| D: delay | D0 → D1 | 5 → 9 | How does an unexpected 15-minute PT delay affect mode choice? |
| R: roadworks | R0 → R1 | 10 → 4 | Does additional car travel time shift demand to other modes? |

The original design notes describe these as matched pairs with four-to-six task-position separation and hypothesize weak fare response, strong weather aversion and diversion from delayed modes. The contrasts are finite changes under the displayed tasks; no causal population elasticity or removal of memory bias is established by task spacing alone.
